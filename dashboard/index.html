How this board is made

The board ranks 198 protocols that record no token in the snapshot and are not a wrapped, staked or bridged product of one that trades: the snapshot's 40-protocol watchlist, plus every other protocol that passes the same screen, large or small. The other 560 protocols in the snapshot are not ranked. The ones whose token already trades are listed apart, at the market's own values.

Implied value answers one question: if this protocol issued a token today and the market priced it the way it prices the protocol's trading peers, what would that token be worth? It is a conditional value read off the protocol's own record, not a forecast that a token is coming, and not a promise about when.

Start with the price of a dollar of TVL. Of the snapshot's 758 protocols, 128 record both a market cap and a TVL. In each category, the median of market cap over TVL across those peers is what the market currently pays for a dollar of that kind of TVL. A category with few trading peers is easy to fool: one peer in this snapshot sits at 400 times its TVL, a small float under a large cap. So each peer ratio is capped between 0.02 and 4 before any median is taken, and every category median is then blended with the global peer median of 0.223 in proportion to how few peers stand behind it: a category with fifteen peers keeps nearly all of its own median, a category with one peer mostly borrows the global one. A category with no trading peers at all prices at the global median, and the board says so on every such row.

Three adjustments follow, all from the protocol's own record. Growth: the seven-day change in TVL, clamped between minus 60 and plus 120 percent, moves the value by half the change, a factor between 0.70 and 1.60; a protocol whose change the snapshot does not report counts as unchanged. Maturity: a protocol that has run longer with no token prices closer to its peers, a factor from 0.60 for a listing the snapshot has just picked up to 1.00 at three years; a protocol with no listing date counts as one year old. Breadth: each chain beyond the first adds three percent, up to a factor of 1.24, because a product already running on more chains carries more of a network with it into any launch.

Every figure on this page is computed from the snapshot's ten fields when the board is built: the peer count behind each category, each factor, each product. No protocol is named anywhere in the model; one function prices every row, and the detail view of any row shows the whole multiplication. The model's known weak points are stated with it: a one-week growth window is noisy, TVL is not revenue, and some categories rest on very few peers. The numbers are a ranking instrument. They order the board; they do not appraise any protocol.
